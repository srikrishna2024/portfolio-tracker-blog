Why Your SIP Returns Are Lower Than the Fund's Returns

You chose a fund that delivered 14% CAGR over 10 years. Your XIRR is 9.8%. You did not make a mistake with the fund. You made a mistake with yourself.

The gap has a name

The difference between what a fund delivers and what its investors actually earn is called the behaviour gap. It is one of the most well-documented phenomena in personal finance, and it applies to every market and every generation of investors.

The fund delivered 14%. You got 9.8%. The missing 4.2% did not go anywhere. It was earned by investors who stayed in the fund when you were out of it.

How the gap forms: the four patterns

1

You started SIPs after the fund had already run up

Many investors discover a fund after it has had two or three good years and appears prominently in performance tables. They start SIPs at a higher NAV. When the fund inevitably underperforms the next year, they experience losses that long-term holders did not.

2

You paused or stopped SIPs during corrections

This is the single most expensive mistake in SIP investing. A 30% market correction feels catastrophic. Stopping the SIP feels like self-preservation. But a correction is exactly when each SIP buys the most units. The investors who earn the fund's return are the ones who kept buying through the fall. The ones who stopped missed the cheapest months.

3

You added lump sums at market peaks

Bonus season or a windfall often coincides with market enthusiasm. Investors who add lump sums when markets are near highs anchor a significant portion of their investment at elevated NAVs. This creates a long period of apparent underperformance that feels frustrating even when the fund is doing exactly what it should.

4

You redeemed during the recovery

Markets fall, you hold. Markets start to recover, relief sets in. At some point in the recovery, you redeem to "lock in" the partial recovery. Then the market continues upward without you. You experienced the full drawdown and only a fraction of the recovery. This is the most painful version of the behaviour gap.

Why this happens even to informed investors

The behaviour gap is not caused by ignorance. It is caused by the absence of a framework that answers the question "should I be worried right now?"

When markets fall 25% and you have no way to assess whether your retirement goal is still on track, every instinct says act. The action feels protective. It is only in retrospect that you can see it was destructive.

When you have a goal-based framework, the question changes. You are no longer asking "is the market down?". You are asking "is my retirement goal still achievable given this fall?". For a goal 18 years away, a 25% fall in year 4 typically does not derail the goal. Knowing that changes how the fall feels. And how it feels determines what you do.

The only reliable solution

There is no investment product, no fund selection methodology, and no market timing strategy that reliably closes the behaviour gap. The only thing that works is automating the behaviour you want and removing the decisions that cause harm.

Automated SIPs on a fixed date, every month, regardless of what the market is doing. A clear goal-based framework that tells you whether to be concerned. An annual review instead of monthly monitoring. These are not exciting. They are effective.

The investors who earn close to the fund's actual return are the ones who found a way to make inaction the default. The portfolio runs. Life goes on. The compounding happens in the background.

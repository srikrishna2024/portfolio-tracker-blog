Why Privacy Matters for Your Financial Data

Your portfolio data is more revealing than you might think. Here is why keeping it on your own machine is not paranoia. It is prudence.

What your portfolio data reveals

A complete record of your mutual fund transactions reveals more about you than almost any other dataset:

💼
Your income level

SIP amounts and lump sum investments signal earnings and savings rate.

🏠
Your life stage

Goals, timelines, and redemptions reveal whether you are accumulating, buying a house, or funding education.

😰
Your stress responses

Whether you stopped SIPs in March 2020 or added more reveals your actual risk tolerance under pressure.

🎯
Your goals and timeline

Retirement corpus targets, education planning, and house down payments map your entire life plan.

This is exactly the kind of data that financial product companies, insurers, lenders, and marketers want. It allows precise targeting for products you may not need, at prices calibrated to what you can afford.

What happens to your data on cloud portfolio apps

When you upload your CAMS statement to a cloud portfolio app, several things may happen depending on their terms of service:

  • It is stored on their servers. If they are breached, your complete financial history is exposed. India has seen multiple fintech data breaches in recent years.
  • It may be used to train models. Many services reserve the right to use anonymised data for product improvement, risk modelling, or analytics.
  • It may be sold or shared. If the company is acquired, merged, or shuts down, data handling terms can change overnight.
  • It disappears with the service. Several well-funded Indian fintech portfolio trackers have shut down or been acquired, taking user data with them. Years of transaction history, gone.

The local-first difference

Personal Portfolio Tracker stores everything in a database on your own computer. There is no server to breach. There is no company holding your data. There is no terms-of-service change that affects you.

🔍

Verifiable, not promised

The Privacy Audit page inside the software shows you live network connections while it runs. You can watch in real time and confirm nothing is leaving your machine. This is proof, not a privacy policy.

📴

Works fully offline

All analysis, calculations, and goal tracking work without an internet connection. The software only needs the internet to download fund prices from AMFI's public website, and you control when that happens.

🗄️

Your backup, your control

Your data backup is a file you create and store wherever you choose. Google Drive, an external drive, an encrypted USB. No automatic sync to any service you did not choose.

🤖

AI is strictly opt-in

AI features are off by default. If you enable them, only fund names, values, and goal amounts are sent. Account numbers, PAN, and bank details are never stored in the database to begin with, so they cannot be transmitted.

What about financial advisors and platforms?

Sharing data with a SEBI-registered advisor you trust is different. You are choosing to share, you know who receives it, and there is a regulatory framework. That is a considered decision.

The risk with cloud portfolio apps is that you are sharing with a company whose primary business is not financial advice. Their business model may depend on your data in ways that are not immediately obvious from the product experience.

Privacy as a feature, not a constraint

Some people treat privacy tools as something only people with something to hide need. That gets it backwards. Financial privacy is a basic element of financial security. You do not share your salary with strangers. You do not leave your bank statements on a coffee shop table. Keeping your portfolio data on your own machine is the same instinct applied to software.

Personal Portfolio Tracker was built on this assumption from the start. Privacy is not a setting you toggle on. It is the default architecture.

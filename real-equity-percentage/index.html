Your Real Equity Percentage Is Probably Lower Than You Think

Most mutual fund investors believe they are 75 to 80% equity. When EPF, NPS, and PPF are included, the real number is often 50 to 60%. This changes every decision that follows.

What most portfolio tools show you

Open any mutual fund portfolio tracker and it will show you your equity-to-debt split based on your mutual fund holdings. If you have ₹30 lakhs in equity funds and ₹5 lakhs in debt funds, it will report 86% equity.

That number is accurate for your mutual fund portfolio. It is not accurate for your actual wealth.

What is missing

For most salaried investors in India, the full picture includes instruments that never appear in a CAMS or MF Central statement:

🚫 Invisible to most tools
  • EPF — earns 8.15% guaranteed, effectively a high-yield debt instrument. For a 40-year-old employee, this corpus is often ₹20 to 40 lakhs.
  • NPS — even if you chose 75% equity in your NPS, the full corpus including the debt portion is invisible to your mutual fund tracker.
  • PPF — 15-year lock-in, government-guaranteed, fully debt. A consistent PPF investor may have ₹10 to 25 lakhs here.
  • Superannuation — employer contribution to a group annuity. Often completely forgotten and entirely debt.

A concrete example

Consider an investor with this portfolio:

InstrumentValueType
Equity mutual funds₹35 lakhsEquity
Debt mutual funds₹5 lakhsDebt
EPF corpus₹28 lakhsDebt
NPS Tier I (75E/25C)₹12 lakhs₹9L equity, ₹3L debt
PPF₹8 lakhsDebt
Total₹88 lakhs

Mutual fund tracker shows: 87% equity (35/40).

Actual allocation: equity ₹44L (35 + 9), debt ₹44L (5 + 28 + 3 + 8) = 50% equity, 50% debt.

The investor who thought they were aggressively invested in equity is actually at a balanced allocation. Their decision to add more equity funds to "increase equity exposure" may be unnecessary or counterproductive once the full picture is visible.

Why this matters for decisions

Every asset allocation decision you make based on only your mutual fund portfolio is based on incomplete information. Common mistakes that result:

  • Adding more equity funds when the full portfolio is already adequately allocated
  • Thinking you need more debt funds when EPF already provides significant debt exposure
  • Miscalculating the SIP required for retirement because the EPF corpus is not in the projection
  • Setting a glide path for retirement that does not account for EPF's guaranteed return and its contribution to the debt bucket

None of these mistakes are visible until EPF, NPS, and PPF are in the same view as your mutual funds.

EPF as your debt anchor

EPF earns 8.15% tax-free on maturity. It is one of the best debt instruments available to salaried investors, better than most debt mutual funds on a post-tax basis. Most investors underweight its role in their portfolio because they cannot see it alongside their mutual funds.

Once EPF is visible, many investors realise they do not need significant debt mutual fund exposure at all. Their equity mutual fund allocation can be higher because the debt exposure is already covered by EPF. The optimal strategy for many salaried investors is: pure equity mutual funds for growth, EPF as the debt anchor, and no separate debt mutual fund SIP until EPF exposure falls below the target debt allocation.

SIP Step-Up: The Most Powerful Lever You Have

Most investors set a SIP amount once and forget it. Increasing it by 10% each year changes the retirement outcome more than any other decision.

What a step-up SIP does to the numbers

Start with ₹10,000 per month at age 28. Retire at 58. Assume 12% CAGR throughout.

StrategySIP at year 30Corpus at 58
Flat ₹10,000 forever₹10,000~₹3.5 crore
Step up 5% every year₹43,200~₹7.2 crore
Step up 10% every year₹1,74,500~₹15.8 crore

The 10% step-up produces a corpus 4.5 times larger than the flat SIP, with the same starting amount and the same fund. The extra corpus comes entirely from investing more as income grows, not from any change in the investment itself.

Why most people do not do this

The obstacle is not financial. It is behavioural. Income grows, but so does lifestyle. A raise arrives, and within six months the higher income feels normal. The SIP stays at the old level because no one ever updated it.

The solution is a rule, not willpower: every time take-home pay increases, increase the SIP before adjusting lifestyle spending. Even stepping up by half the increment of any raise captures most of the benefit.

A ₹15,000 raise per month. Step up the SIP by ₹7,500. The remaining ₹7,500 goes to lifestyle. This is sustainable and still captures enormous long-term benefit.

Step-up and goal adequacy

The most important use of SIP step-up is closing the gap on a goal that is falling behind. When Personal Portfolio Tracker shows that your retirement goal needs ₹4,200 more per month to stay on track, the answer is not to switch funds. It is to step up the SIP.

Fund switching involves tax, friction, and the risk of moving at the wrong time. SIP step-up involves nothing more than increasing a standing instruction. It is the cleanest lever available.

The software shows you the exact SIP required for each goal to be on track. When there is a gap, the step-up amount is the first and usually the best response.

When to step up and by how much

  • Annual raise: step up by at least 50% of the take-home increment
  • Bonus: deploy a lump sum directly into the goal that is furthest behind
  • Goal review: every April, check whether current SIP is sufficient; if not, calculate the increment needed and set it up immediately
  • New income stream: any new income source (freelance, rental, part-time) should go to goals before it gets absorbed into expenses

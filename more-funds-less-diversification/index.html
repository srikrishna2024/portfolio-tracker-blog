More Funds Does Not Mean More Diversification

Investors add funds thinking they are spreading risk. Often they are concentrating it. The number of funds in a portfolio has almost nothing to do with how diversified it is.

The illusion of diversification

A portfolio with twelve mutual funds feels diversified. It rarely is. If eight of those twelve funds are large-cap equity funds — different names, different AMCs, similar mandates — they hold largely overlapping stocks. When the Nifty 50 falls 25%, all eight fall roughly together. The portfolio has twelve line items and one risk exposure.

True diversification means the assets in a portfolio do not all respond to the same market event in the same direction at the same time. By that definition, two funds with a correlation of 0.95 are not two diversification sources. They are one fund with extra paperwork.

How to measure whether your portfolio is actually diversified

Correlation is the right tool, not fund count. Two funds are truly diversifying each other only when their return series move somewhat independently — one can be up while the other is flat or down. A correlation above 0.85 between two funds in the same category usually means you have duplication, not diversification.

The Correlation page in Personal Portfolio Tracker shows a heatmap of correlation between all your holdings over 1Y, 3Y, and 5Y windows. Switch to the 3Y view to see through a full market cycle. Pairs above 0.85 are candidates for consolidation — you are not getting the diversification benefit you expect from holding both.

The Factor Analysis page takes this further: it shows tracking error and R² against the benchmark for each fund. A fund with R² above 0.95 moves almost entirely with the index. If you hold three such funds, you effectively hold one index fund three times — with three sets of expense ratios.

What real diversification looks like

1

Asset class diversification

Equity and debt respond differently to the same economic events. A rate rise hurts equity valuations but benefits short-duration debt. Holding equity funds alongside a short-duration debt fund or PPF is genuine diversification. Holding five equity funds is not.

2

Category diversification within equity

Large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap funds respond differently to market cycles. Small-caps tend to fall more in crashes but recover faster. Adding a genuine mid-cap or small-cap fund alongside a large-cap fund does add diversification — they are not highly correlated over full cycles. Adding a second large-cap fund adds nothing.

3

Style diversification

Value and growth styles go through long cycles of relative outperformance. A portfolio that holds only growth-oriented funds was exposed to the 2022 rate-hike correction in a specific way. Mixing a value-oriented fund with growth exposure reduces style concentration without adding many funds.

4

Goal-based separation

The most practical diversification is matching fund risk to goal timeline. A fund for a goal 20 years away can be 85% equity. A fund for a goal 3 years away should not be. Holding different funds for different goals, at appropriate risk levels, is better diversification than holding many similar funds for a single goal.

The right number of funds

For most investors with two to four distinct financial goals, three to five funds cover the full requirement: one or two large-cap or index funds, one genuine mid-cap fund, and one short-duration debt or hybrid fund for near-term goals. That structure has low correlation between components, low total expense ratio, and is simple enough to actually review once a year.

Beyond five funds, additional diversification benefit is marginal. The correlation between the sixth and seventh fund and what you already hold is typically very high. What grows is complexity, not protection.

The Correlation page will show you this directly on your own portfolio. If most of your fund pairs are dark red (highly correlated), consolidation to fewer better-chosen funds will not increase risk — it will reduce noise without reducing protection.

AMC concentration is a separate risk

Diversification across fund categories does not automatically mean diversification across fund houses. Two funds from the same AMC share the same investment team culture, the same risk management processes, and potentially the same blind spots. If an AMC has a governance issue or an investment philosophy that proves systematically wrong in a particular cycle, all its funds are affected together.

The Goal Diagnostics page in Personal Portfolio Tracker shows K7 — AMC concentration across your goal's fund mix. A high K7 score means a large portion of your goal is exposed to a single AMC's performance. Spreading across two or three AMCs costs nothing and eliminates this specific concentration.

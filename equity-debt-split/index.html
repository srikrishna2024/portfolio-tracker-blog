What Is the Right Equity-Debt Split for You?

Not 60/40. Not 80/20. The right allocation depends on your goal timeline, not a generic rule of thumb.

Why generic rules fail

The most common allocation advice you will hear: subtract your age from 100 to get your equity percentage. At 35, keep 65% equity. At 50, keep 50% equity.

This rule has two problems. First, it treats your entire portfolio as one thing working toward one undifferentiated goal. Second, it uses age as a proxy for risk tolerance when the actual driver is how long you have before you need the money.

A 50-year-old with a retirement goal 15 years away and a house purchase goal 2 years away needs very different allocations for each. Applying 50% equity to everything is wrong for both goals simultaneously.

The right framework: per-goal allocation

Each goal has its own timeline and its own required return. The allocation for that goal should reflect both. A simple evidence-based starting point:

Years to goalEquityDebtRationale
More than 15 years80%20%Long recovery time. Equity volatility works in your favour through SIP averaging.
10 to 15 years70%30%Still growth-oriented but beginning to build a safety buffer.
5 to 10 years60%40%Goal is close enough that a bad market sequence would hurt.
3 to 5 years40%60%Capital preservation starts to matter more than growth.
Under 3 years20%80%No time to recover from a correction. Protect the corpus.

These are starting points, not rules. A goal where the corpus is already much larger than the target can afford more conservative allocation. A goal that is significantly underfunded may need more equity to have a chance of closing the gap.

What this means in practice

A 38-year-old investor might have three active goals:

🎯
Retirement (22 years away)

80% equity appropriate. Long horizon means time to absorb volatility and benefit from compounding.

🎓
Child's education (10 years away)

60% equity. Goal is approaching. Start building the debt buffer now, not in year 8.

🏠
House down payment (3 years away)

20% equity maximum. This money needs to be there on a specific date regardless of market conditions.

Each goal has a different allocation. The same mid-cap fund that is appropriate for retirement is inappropriate for the house down payment. Mapping funds to goals is what makes this distinction visible and actionable.

The portfolio-wide number is misleading

When you look at your overall portfolio equity percentage, you are seeing a weighted average across all your goals. That number has limited meaning. A portfolio that is 65% equity might be perfectly allocated for a retirement goal 20 years away and dangerously over-allocated for a goal 2 years away that is lumped in with it.

The useful question is never "what is my overall equity percentage?" It is "for each goal, is the allocation appropriate for the time I have left?"

The Indian Investor's Blind Spot

Most portfolio tools are built for mutual funds only. But the average Indian investor's wealth is split across six or more instruments. Here is what gets missed.

The complete picture most tools ignore

Think about where your money actually is. For a typical salaried Indian investor:

👁️ Visible to most tools
  • Equity mutual funds (CAMS / MF Central)
  • Debt mutual funds (CAMS / MF Central)
  • ELSS funds (CAMS / MF Central)
🚫 Invisible to most tools
  • EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)
  • NPS (National Pension System, Tier I and II)
  • PPF (Public Provident Fund)
  • Superannuation (employer contribution)
  • Stocks and equity
  • Fixed deposits and bonds

For many investors, EPF alone represents 30 to 40 percent of their total retirement wealth. If your portfolio tool cannot see it, every retirement projection it generates is wrong by that same margin.

The EPF problem

EPF is compulsory for most salaried employees, accumulates for decades, and is often the single largest financial asset an investor holds. Yet it appears in no standard portfolio tracker because it is not accessible through CAMS or MF Central.

The result: most investors have no idea what their real equity-to-debt split is. A portfolio that looks 80% equity in their mutual fund tracker might actually be 55% equity once EPF, PPF, and NPS are included. That changes the entire retirement picture and the glide path decisions that flow from it.

Personal Portfolio Tracker imports EPF passbook data directly from the UMANG app PDF. Your full balance history, contributions, and employer contributions land in the same view as your mutual funds.

NPS: the invisible retirement corpus

NPS Tier I is locked until retirement and often treated as a set-and-forget contribution. But it compounds over decades and its allocation between equity (E), corporate bonds (C), and government securities (G) directly affects your retirement corpus.

Most investors cannot answer these questions about their NPS:

  • What is the current corpus split between E, C, and G?
  • What is the XIRR on the NPS contributions since inception?
  • How does the NPS corpus affect the equity-debt balance in my retirement goal?
  • Is my auto-choice scheme's glide path appropriate for my timeline?

Personal Portfolio Tracker imports your NSDL CRA statement and answers all of these questions in context with the rest of your portfolio.

PPF: the long-duration debt anchor

PPF is 15-year lock-in, government-guaranteed, tax-free on maturity. It is one of the best debt instruments available to Indian investors. It is also completely invisible to every mutual fund tracker.

If you are counting on PPF as part of your retirement corpus or emergency reserve, it needs to appear in your retirement projections. Personal Portfolio Tracker imports your PPF statement and projects the balance forward at the current guaranteed rate.

Superannuation: the forgotten benefit

Many corporate employees have a superannuation benefit from their employer that they barely think about. It is typically invested in a group annuity or a fund with your employer's chosen insurer. The statements are annual and easy to miss.

Personal Portfolio Tracker supports superannuation statement imports from major providers. The balance appears in your debt portfolio and feeds into your retirement projection.

What the full picture changes

Once EPF, NPS, PPF, and Superannuation are visible alongside your mutual funds, several things shift:

  • Your actual equity percentage is typically lower than your mutual fund tracker suggests
  • Your retirement corpus projection becomes significantly more accurate
  • The required SIP to bridge the gap changes accordingly
  • Your asset allocation decisions for new investments need to account for the existing debt exposure in these instruments

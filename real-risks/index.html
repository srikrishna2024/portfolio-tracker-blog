The Real Risks Are Not Market Volatility

Most investors are acutely aware of the risk they can see on a chart. The risks that actually destroy retirement plans are quieter and less visible.

The risk you worry about vs the risks that matter

What investors worry about

  • Short-term market volatility
  • A fund underperforming its benchmark by 1-2%
  • Missing a quarterly rally
  • Picking the "wrong" fund category

These feel urgent. They are rarely catastrophic for long-term investors.

What actually destroys retirement plans

  • Inflation silently eroding purchasing power over 25 years
  • Outliving your corpus (longevity risk)
  • A market crash in the 3 years before or after retirement
  • Underinvesting for decades because SIPs were never stepped up
  • Stopping investments during corrections and missing the recovery

These feel abstract. They are the ones that actually matter.

Inflation: the silent compounding in reverse

Inflation does not feel dangerous because it works slowly. But at 6% per year, the purchasing power of ₹1 crore is reduced to ₹55 lakhs in 10 years, ₹31 lakhs in 20 years, and ₹17 lakhs in 30 years.

An investor who retires with ₹2 crore at 60 and expects to live to 85 needs that corpus to last 25 years. At 6% inflation and 5% withdrawal rate, the corpus may run out well before 85, even if invested conservatively. Most retirement calculators understate this because they use today's expense as the base, not the inflation-adjusted expense at retirement.

The correct way to think about inflation is not as a background percentage but as a second tax on savings, levied continuously and compounding against you.

Longevity risk: the one no one plans for

Life expectancy in India has risen significantly and continues to rise. A couple retiring at 60 today should plan for at least one of them living to 90. That is a 30-year retirement. Most retirement plans are built for 20.

The consequences of outliving your corpus are severe. You cannot go back to work at 82. You cannot take more risk at 85 to recover lost ground. The only solution is to build a larger corpus and invest it in a way that can sustain withdrawals for three decades, not two.

Sequence of returns risk: the retirement destroyer

Sequence of returns risk is the risk that bad market returns occur at the worst possible time: the few years immediately before or after retirement.

Consider two investors with identical 25-year average returns of 11%. Investor A gets strong early returns and weak late returns. Investor B gets weak early returns and strong late returns. If both are accumulating throughout, they end at the same place. But if Investor A is withdrawing during the weak late returns, the corpus depletes permanently. Investor B, who had weak early years while accumulating, is fine.

This is why the allocation shift near retirement matters so much. High equity allocation is appropriate when you have time to recover from bad sequences. As retirement approaches, shifting toward more stable assets reduces the exposure to a catastrophic sequence.

What to actually do about these risks

These risks have practical responses:

  • Inflation: use inflation-adjusted targets in your retirement plan, not today's costs. Personal Portfolio Tracker applies inflation automatically in goal projections.
  • Longevity: plan to age 90 as a default, not 75. If you die earlier, your family benefits. If you live longer, you are not stranded.
  • Sequence of returns: shift allocation toward debt and stable assets in the 5 years approaching each major goal. The glide path built into the software tracks this per goal, not just for the whole portfolio.
  • Underinvestment: step up SIPs with every income increase. The biggest risk for most mid-career investors is not investing enough, not investing in the wrong fund.

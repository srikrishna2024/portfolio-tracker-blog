The Annual Review: One Hour a Year Is Enough

Most investors either check their portfolio every day or never review it at all. One structured hour each April is more valuable than either extreme.

Why April

The Indian financial year ends on 31 March. By the first week of April, you have a complete picture: a full year of LTCG and STCG crystallised, your EPF and NPS annual statements available, fresh CAMS and MF Central consolidated account statements, and a clear view of how the year's market performance affected your goals.

Doing the review in April also gives you time to act on what you find before the next financial year gets underway — stepping up SIPs, harvesting gains within the exemption limit, or adjusting allocation before the next year of compounding begins.

The one-hour checklist

This is the complete sequence. Each step should take 5 to 10 minutes. None of it requires looking at daily market movements or fund rankings.

1

Import updated statements

10 min

Download your latest CAS from CAMS or MF Central. Download your EPF passbook from UMANG. Update your NPS statement from NSDL. Import all of them. Your portfolio is now current to end of March.

2

Check goal progress

10 min

Open Goal Dashboard. For each goal: is the projected corpus still on track? Has the gap grown or shrunk compared to last April? If a goal has fallen behind by more than 10%, that needs action. If it is on track, note it and move on.

3

Review SIP adequacy

5 min

The software shows the required monthly SIP for each goal. Compare it to what you are actually investing. If there is a gap, this is the moment to close it. A salary review in the past year is the natural source — step up by at least half of any income increase before lifestyle inflation absorbs it.

4

Check fund reliability

10 min

Look at K4 (tracking error) and K8 (safety margin) for each goal. A fund that has been in Watch or Switch zone for two consecutive annual reviews needs a decision. One bad year is noise. Two consecutive years is a pattern. The Goal Diagnostics page shows the trend.

5

Review allocation vs glide path

5 min

As goals get closer, equity allocation should reduce. Check whether any goal has drifted significantly from its recommended equity-debt split. A goal that is 3 years away should not be 80% equity. The Goal Dashboard shows current vs recommended allocation for each goal.

6

LTCG harvesting

10 min

You have a ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption on LTCG from equity funds. If you have not used it, identify units you can redeem and immediately reinvest to step up the cost basis. The Tax Optimizer page in the software identifies exactly which units to redeem for maximum benefit with minimum tax.

7

Take a backup

2 min

After all updates and changes, take a fresh backup from Admin and copy it to Google Drive or OneDrive. Your April backup is your complete financial snapshot for the year — keep it permanently.

What you do not need to do in the annual review

  • Look at 1-year fund performance rankings — too short a window to be meaningful
  • Compare your portfolio to someone else's — your goals and timeline are different
  • Switch funds because a new category is doing well — that is performance chasing
  • Add more funds — complexity does not improve returns
  • Worry about short-term market movements — the annual review is a 12-month lens, not a daily one

The compounding effect of doing this consistently

An investor who does a proper annual review in April every year and adjusts SIPs accordingly will, over a 20-year period, significantly outperform one who either ignores their portfolio entirely or over-manages it. The review keeps goals calibrated to reality. The SIP step-ups compound. The glide path shifts happen on schedule. Fund problems are caught in year two, not year five.

One hour a year is not the minimum viable effort. For a well-constructed portfolio, it is the optimal effort. More frequent intervention tends to introduce noise rather than signal.

How Much Is Enough? Setting Your Retirement Number

Most people underestimate how much they need for retirement. Not by a small amount. Often by 40 to 60 percent. Here is why, and how to get the number right.

Why most retirement estimates are wrong

The most common way people estimate their retirement corpus is: current monthly expenses multiplied by 12, multiplied by the number of retirement years. If you spend ₹80,000 per month today and plan to retire for 25 years, that gives ₹2.4 crore.

This estimate is wrong for three reasons:

📊

Inflation is not accounted for

₹80,000 today will need ₹2.7 lakhs per month to buy the same things in 25 years at 5% inflation. The corpus must fund inflated expenses, not today's expenses.

📅

The timeline is too short

Retirement at 60 and living to 85 is a 25-year retirement. Many people plan for 20. At 85 and out of money, there are no good options.

💰

Corpus must stay invested

A ₹2.4 crore corpus parked in a savings account at 3.5% will not sustain 25 years of withdrawals with inflation. The corpus must be invested at a return that exceeds inflation throughout retirement.

The correct calculation

The proper retirement corpus calculation has four inputs:

  1. Current monthly expenses: what you spend today in today's rupees
  2. Years to retirement: the time available to accumulate the corpus
  3. Inflation rate: typically 5 to 6% for Indian lifestyle costs
  4. Retirement duration: from retirement age to target life expectancy, typically 25 to 30 years

With these inputs, the corpus required is the present value at retirement of all future inflation-adjusted expenses, discounted at the expected post-retirement portfolio return.

The gap most people do not know they have

Run the calculation above and compare it to your current projected corpus based on existing savings and SIPs. For most people in their 30s and 40s, the gap is large and the required monthly SIP to close it is significantly higher than what they are currently investing.

This is not cause for panic. It is cause for clarity. Seeing the actual gap is the first step to closing it. And the earlier you see it, the easier it is to close with manageable increments to monthly SIP rather than desperate last-decade catch-up investing.

What changes the number most

Three levers move the required corpus significantly:

  • Working longer: retiring at 62 instead of 58 adds 4 years of accumulation and removes 4 years of withdrawal. The corpus required can drop by 20 to 30% for this change alone.
  • Post-retirement income: part-time consulting, rental income, or any income that supplements the corpus during retirement dramatically reduces the drawdown rate and extends corpus longevity.
  • Expense management at retirement: a retired household with grown children, no mortgage, and no commuting costs often spends 20 to 30% less than a mid-career household. Using retirement-stage expenses rather than current expenses in the calculation gives a more accurate number.

Why this calculation must be revisited annually

The retirement number is not a one-time calculation. It changes every year as your expenses evolve, your timeline shortens, and your current corpus grows. Running the calculation once at 35 and never reviewing it is one of the most common planning mistakes.
